# How Do I Connect with Other Homeschool Families?

Famlo connects families by putting them in the same room. It helps you find the co-ops, classes and park days near you, book your family in, and keep in touch with the organizations running them. The friendships happen at the activities.

There is no feed to scroll and no posts to like. On Famlo, connection is a schedule you actually keep.

## Quick answer

Join two or three organizations that fit your family, book the things they run, and go to the same ones again and again. On an organization's page, use **Message** to ask questions before you commit, and **Follow** to hear when it posts something new. Set up **Location alerts** to hear about new activities in your area.

## What Famlo has, and what it doesn't

Knowing this up front saves you looking for a button that isn't there.

**What you can do:**

- Browse organizations and activities, and follow, join or ask to join an organization.
- Book activities and see them all on your calendar.
- Message organizations in one inbox, and read their announcements there.
- Keep track of what's coming with **Follow**, **Saved** and **Location alerts**.
- Write a testimonial about an organization for other families to read.
- Report a message, or block someone from messaging you one-to-one.

**What isn't there:**

- No feed, no posts, no comments and no photo tagging.
- No directory of families, and no way to start a conversation with a family you've never messaged.
- No public family profile. Your family's details are seen only by the organizations you join or book with.

## Step 1: Join organizations that fit

The most useful thing you can do is choose well and choose few.

1. Go to famlo.co/places.
2. Use **What** to search by name or pick an **Organization type**, such as Co-ops.
3. Use **Category** to find an educational approach or a faith affiliation, when organizations nearby have one.
4. Use **Ages & more** to set an age range, **Free to join** or **Paid**, and the days it **Meets on**.

Before you join, read the organization's page. What it runs and how often tells you more about the community than any description. Its **Staff** and **What families say** sections help too.

<Screenshot src="/img/screens/families/connect-families-org-buttons.webp" alt="An organization's page with the Follow, Message and Request to join buttons numbered 1 to 3" url="famlo.co/co-op/quillwood-learning-co-op" width={1280} height={900} />

The buttons at the top of the page do different jobs:

- **Follow** (1) tells you when the organization posts a new public activity. It isn't joining.
- **Message** (2) starts a conversation with the organization.
- **Join** (3) makes you a member. Some organizations show **Request to join** instead, and review each request first. A group that charges dues always reviews requests and shows the price, for example **Request to join · $50.22 / month**.

The full walkthrough is in [Finding organizations and reading their page](/docs/families/organizations).

:::tip Two or three, not eight
Depth beats breadth. Seeing the same six families every week for a term builds more than turning up once at eight different groups. Pick one weekly commitment and one monthly one, and leave room.
:::

## Step 2: Fill in your family

This isn't a social profile, and other parents don't browse it. It's what an organizer needs on the day, and filling it in once makes every booking faster.

1. Select **Family** at the top of famlo.co, or the **Family** tab on a phone.
2. Select **+ Add family member**, then fill in **Full name**, **Relationship** and **Birthdate**, and select **Add**.
3. Open each child's **Health & safety** page and work through the rows, such as **Allergies**, **Pickup & photos** and **Emergency contact**.

Only the organizations your family joins or books that child with can see these details, and other parents can't. See [Your family and children](/docs/families/your-family).

## Step 3: Book, and then show up

Booking is where a connection stops being theoretical. Open an activity and select **RSVP** for a free one or **Register** for a paid one. Choose which of your children are coming, and confirm. See [Registering and checking out](/docs/families/registering).

Your bookings collect on [your calendar](/docs/families/calendar), where **Who's going** and **Communities** let you see one child or one organization at a time.

The activities that build friendships are the repeating ones. A weekly class puts your family in front of the same families every week. A one-off field trip does it once.

| Kind of activity | What it gives you |
| --- | --- |
| Co-op classes and weekly clubs | The same families, every week. The strongest option. |
| Field trips | Hours of easy, unstructured time together |
| Park days and meetups | Low commitment, easy for a first visit |
| Camps and workshops | A lot of time together over a few days |
| Drop-ins | A way to try a group before you join it |

## Step 4: Use Messages

Everything you can reply to on Famlo lands in one inbox. On a computer, select the bell in the header. On a phone, tap **Messages** in the bar at the bottom.

**To ask an organization something,** open its page and select **Message**. Your question goes to the organization, not to one person, so it doesn't wait for one volunteer to come back. Answers show the organization's name.

**To ask about an activity you booked,** open its row in your inbox and type in the box at the bottom, which reads "Ask a question…". The line above the box confirms that replies are private between you and the organization.

**To start a new conversation,** select **New conversation** (the pencil) and type at least two letters of a name. Search finds organizations by name, and people you already have a one-to-one conversation with.

<Screenshot src="/img/screens/families/connect-families-new-conversation.webp" alt="The New Conversation box with Sunnyside typed in the search field and one result, Sunnyside Learning Co., labeled Organization" url="famlo.co/messages" width={449} height={204} />

Search never finds families you haven't talked to before, so you can't start a new conversation with another family on Famlo. The way to meet someone is still to turn up and say hello.

To react to a message, select **…** next to it and pick one of six reactions. The same menu has **Report message**. In a one-to-one conversation it also has **Block this person**. See [Messages](/docs/families/messages) for everything else the inbox does.

:::note Health details don't belong in a message
Famlo won't send a message that reads like a health record, such as a line that says "Allergies: peanuts". Put those details in your family's profile instead, where they stay private and only the organizations that need them can see them. Asking "Is Friday's snack nut-free?" is fine.
:::

## Step 5: Keep up with what's coming

**Announcements.** Organizations send announcements to the families booked into an activity, and they can also send one to the whole organization. An organization-wide announcement can go to its members, to everyone booked into any of its activities, or to both. So joining an organization and booking its activities both keep you in the loop. Announcements show in your inbox, on the activity's or the organization's row, and arrive by email unless you turn off **Activity updates** email.

**Follow.** Select **Follow** on an organization's page, and Famlo tells you when it posts a new public activity, in Famlo and by email. It gives you no member access.

**Saved.** Select the heart on an activity to save it. Your saved activities are under **Saved** in the account menu. If an activity is full, the button reads **Join the waitlist**, and saving it means you're alerted when a spot opens. The first family to register gets the spot. When an organization announces something about an activity, it can choose to include families who saved it.

**Location alerts.** These watch an area for activities that don't exist yet. Open **Location alerts** from the account menu, choose a **State**, narrow it by city, category, type, ages or keywords if you like, and select **Save alert**. See [Location alerts and area watches](/docs/families/discover/location-alerts).

**Organizations' own emails.** Some organizations show an optional **Email me updates** box when you register. Ticking it adds you to their mailing list. They send those emails themselves, not Famlo.

## Step 6: Share your experience

When a class or a membership has gone well, say so. A testimonial helps the next family decide, and it tells the organizers what's working.

1. On the organization's page, go to **What families say** and select **Share your experience**.
2. Choose what it's about, write what you'd tell another family, and choose how your name appears.
3. Choose whether the organization may publish it, then send it.

Nothing is published unless you allow it and the organization chooses to. Organizations may also ask you after a class ends, with a **Leave a testimonial** card on your **Dashboard** (**Home** on a phone). See [Share your experience (testimonials)](/docs/families/community/testimonials).

## Building friendships that last

**Be consistent.** Go to the same things. Familiarity comes almost entirely from repetition, and a term of weekly attendance does more than a year of trying things out.

**Be the one who suggests something.** Most groups are full of people waiting for someone else to suggest a park meetup. Be that person once and you won't have to be again.

**Give it a term.** Children and adults both take more than one visit to warm up. Judge a group after six sessions, not after one.

**Volunteer.** Offering to help with an activity is the fastest way from going to a group to belonging to one. Ask the organizers what they need.

## Common challenges

**"I feel like an outsider in an established group."** Go regularly, introduce yourself to the organizers by name, and offer to help with something small. Familiarity does the rest.

**"My children aren't making friends."** Choose activities around their real interests, and arrange something smaller afterwards with one family, rather than hoping the group does it for you.

**"I don't have time."** Commit to one monthly activity you'll really keep, rather than a weekly one you'll drop.

**"Nothing near us matches our approach."** Look a little further out before you lower your standards, and set a location alert for your area. If there's still nothing, start your own. See [How Do I Start a Homeschool Group?](/docs/how-to/start-homeschool-group).

## Troubleshooting

**I can't find a feed or a place to post.** There isn't one. Famlo helps you find activities, book them and talk to the organizations that run them. It isn't a social network.

**I want to message another parent I met at co-op.** Search in **New conversation** only finds organizations and people you already have a conversation with. Swap contact details in person, or ask the organizers to pass a message on.

**I can't find Messages in the account menu.** It isn't there. On a computer, select the bell in the header. On a phone, tap **Messages** at the bottom of the screen.

**I joined an organization and nothing happens.** Check whether it has anything scheduled, and book something. As a member you can get its organization-wide announcements, but the friendships come from turning up.

**I'm not hearing about new activities.** Check that **Activity updates** is on in your [notification settings](/docs/account/notifications), follow the organizations you like, and set up a location alert for your area.

**I asked to join and heard nothing.** The organization's page shows "Your request to join is under review." until its organizers decide. Use **Message** on its page to ask.

**Someone is messaging me and I want it to stop.** Open the conversation, select **…** next to one of their messages, and choose **Block this person**, or **Report message** if it breaks the rules. Famlo's team reviews reported messages. A block covers one-to-one messages only. Organizations you're enrolled with can still message you. See [Report a message or block someone](/docs/families/community/blocking-and-reporting).

**My message won't send and mentions health information.** It reads like a medical record. Rephrase it as an ordinary sentence, or put the detail in your family's profile.
